03 — The step circuit

Steps don't end.
They come back.

The most common misconception about an escalator is that the steps disappear at the top. They don't — they flatten, turn under the comb plate, and travel back along the underside of the truss on the same continuous chain. That return path is where most wear happens, and it's the part nobody inspects.

Carrying run. Steps under load, horizontal to the passenger, climbing at 30°.
Return run. Steps inverted beneath the truss — unloaded, unseen, and where roller wear accumulates.

On a planned maintenance visit we lift the comb plates and inspect the return run directly — step rollers, chain tension, and the sag switch. A step chain that has stretched beyond tolerance is the failure that closes a mall on a Saturday.

05 — Safety devices

Every one gets tested

EN 115 mandates these devices. On a planned visit we function-test each of them and record the result — so when an inspector asks, there is a signed sheet rather than an assurance.

/ 01

Comb impact device

Stops the unit if an object jams between the comb plate and the step tread, in either axis.

/ 02

Step sag switch

Detects a step that has dropped below the tread line before it reaches the comb.

/ 03

Handrail entry guard

Stops the drive if a hand, sleeve or child's finger is drawn into the newel entry point.

/ 04

Step chain safety

Monitors chain elongation and breakage, triggering the brake before the band can run back.

/ 05

Missing step detector

Verifies step presence on the return run and halts before a gap reaches passengers.

/ 06

Overspeed & reversal

Detects speed above 1.2× rated, or unintended reversal of travel direction on the incline.

/ 07

Skirt deflection switch

Trips when a shoe or clothing is trapped in the skirt-to-step clearance.

/ 08

Emergency stop

Accessible push-buttons at both landings, with a controlled brake rather than an abrupt one.
