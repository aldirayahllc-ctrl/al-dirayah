Who we are

An electromechanical contractor for the whole life of a building

Al Dirayah supplies, installs and maintains the systems that make a building work — the lifts and escalators that move people through it, the power and electromechanical plant that runs it, and the low-current networks that connect and protect it.

Scope of work

Vertical transport

Lifts, escalators and moving walkways — installation, repair, modernisation and annual maintenance. Approved subcontractor to TKE, OTIS and KONE.

Power & electromechanical

Electromechanical equipment installation and maintenance, power distribution networks and stations, and power transmission line contracting.

Low current / ELV

CCTV and surveillance, access control, structured cabling and network infrastructure. Subject to ADMCC registration.

Supply & trading

Import and wholesale of lifts, escalators and spare parts, supporting both our own installations and third-party projects.

L02The
name

Meaning and use

Al Dirayah

دِرَايَة — dirāyah — means thorough know-how: the practical mastery that comes from understanding a thing completely. It is not general knowledge. It is the knowledge that lets someone do the work correctly.

That is precisely what a technical contractor sells. Clients do not buy components — they buy the competence to specify, install, commission and maintain them safely. The name states the promise in the language of the market we work in.

Arabic

الدراية

Pronunciation

ad-di-RAA-yah

The d is a sun letter, so the l of “Al” assimilates in Arabic speech. English speakers saying “al di-RAA-yah” are not wrong. Aldar follows the same pattern.

The one rule that matters most

The name is written AL DIRAYAH. Always. Everywhere. By everyone.

Not Diraya. Not Al Diraya. Not Dirayah on its own. Not AlDirayah as one word. Drift usually begins in an email signature or a WhatsApp Business profile and ends up on an invoice, a vendor registration and a site board — after which it is expensive to correct. Both owners are responsible for enforcing this.

The primary line is a direct statement of what the name means, and works in both languages. Use one line per artefact — never both together.

Tone of voice

✓Precise. Give the load rating, the response time, the certification number. Specificity is the proof.
✓Plain. Short sentences. Ordinary words. Our readers are engineers, procurement officers and facility managers reading in a second language.
✓Accountable. Say who does what, by when. Name the standard being met.
✕No superlatives. Not “leading”, “world-class”, “state-of-the-art”. Anyone can type those; nobody believes them.
✕No borrowed prestige. Never imply we are an OEM, or that an OEM endorses us beyond what our agreements permit.

Amber is an accent at roughly 4% of any surface. It marks the one thing that matters on a page — a heading rule, a chevron, a call to action. Large amber fields cheapen it and read as hazard signage rather than brand.

Site and PPE exception

Statutory high-visibility clothing follows EN ISO 20471 and site rules, not this palette. Hi-vis vests, harnesses and helmets stay in their mandated colours. Brand amber is close to hi-vis amber by design, so the two sit together comfortably — but safety specification always wins.

L06Type

Typefaces

Three roles, one engineering voice

All four faces are open-source and free for commercial use, so every subcontractor, printer and signage supplier can install them without a licence negotiation. That matters more than novelty when the same mark has to be produced by ten different vendors.

Display — Barlow Condensed, SemiBold 600

AL DIRAYAH

Lifts · Escalators · Power · Low current

Barlow descends from Californian highway signage — built to be read at distance, narrow enough to fit a van door or a site board. Used for the wordmark, headlines and signage. Uppercase, tracked +10 to +30 units.

Text — IBM Plex Sans, Regular 400 / SemiBold 600

Supply, install and maintain

Every proposal, method statement, maintenance report and pre-qualification document is set in IBM Plex Sans. It was drawn for engineering documentation, holds up at small sizes, and its numerals are unambiguous — which matters when a document carries load ratings and part numbers.

Body 10–11 pt, line height 1.5. Never below 9 pt in print.

L07Bilingual
usage

Arabic and English together

Equal weight, correct order

Everything client-facing in Abu Dhabi is bilingual. Arabic is not a translation bolted on at the end — on government, municipality and developer submissions it is the primary language, and getting the hierarchy wrong is noticed.

Stacked lockup

Rules

✓On bilingual documents, Arabic occupies the right column and English the left, matching reading direction.
✓Set Arabic 1 pt larger than the matching Latin text for optical parity.
✓On government, DMT, ADDC and municipality submissions, Arabic leads.
✓Have a native Arabic speaker proof every set piece before it is printed. Shaping and joining errors are invisible to non-readers and glaring to readers.
✕Never transliterate English marketing copy into Arabic script. Write it in Arabic, or leave it in English.
✕Never use a substitute Arabic font because Plex Arabic was not installed. Fix the installation.
✕Never stretch Arabic glyphs (kashida-padding) to force a line to justify.

Brand rules relating to our OEM principals

Read the agreement before you print anything

Subcontractor agreements with TKE, OTIS and KONE place strict limits on the use of their names, marks and colours. Assume the following unless a specific written permission says otherwise:

  • Never place an OEM logo on our website, vehicles, uniforms, stationery or marketing.
  • Never describe ourselves as “authorised”, “approved” or “official” without the exact wording permitted in writing.
  • Never adopt an OEM’s colours or design language — one reason this identity avoids blue entirely.
  • Client-facing claims about OEM relationships go through a named partner before publication.

The trading activity on our licence — wholesale of lifts, escalators and spare parts — may conflict with exclusivity or genuine-parts clauses in those agreements. Check every clause before trading a single component.

Approval

✓Anything carrying the mark and leaving the company is approved by a named partner before it is produced.
✓Master artwork lives in one place, controlled by the partners. Suppliers receive files from that source only.
✓Supply printers and signage vendors with vector files — SVG, EPS or PDF. Never a screenshot or a PNG lifted from the website.
✕Nobody redraws, re-traces or “tidies” the mark. If a supplier says they need to, they need the vector file instead.
